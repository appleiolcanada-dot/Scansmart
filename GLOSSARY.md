## General Trading Terms

**Ask Price**
The lowest price a seller is willing to accept for a stock.

**Bid Price**
The highest price a buyer is willing to pay for a stock.

**Bid-Ask Spread**
The difference between the bid and ask prices. Smaller spreads indicate higher liquidity.

**Bull Market**
A market condition where prices are rising or expected to rise. Opposite of bear market.

**Bear Market**
A market condition where prices are falling, typically defined as a 20%+ decline from recent highs.

**Day Trading**
Buying and selling securities within the same trading day.

**Due Diligence**
Research and analysis before making an investment decision.

**Going Long**
Buying a stock with the expectation it will increase in value.

**Going Short (Short Selling)**
Selling borrowed shares with the expectation of buying them back at a lower price.

**IPO (Initial Public Offering)**
When a private company first offers shares to the public.

**Liquidity**
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without affecting its price. High volume = high liquidity.

**Market Cap (Market Capitalization)**
Total value of a company's outstanding shares. Price per share × number of shares.
- Mega Cap: $200B+
- Large Cap: $10B - $200B
- Mid Cap: $2B - $10B
- Small Cap: $300M - $2B
- Micro Cap: $50M - $300M
- Nano Cap: Under $50M

**Portfolio**
Collection of investments owned by an individual or institution.

**Position**
The amount of a security owned (long position) or owed (short position).

**Stop Loss**
An order to sell a security when it reaches a certain price, limiting potential losses.

**Take Profit**
An order to sell a security when it reaches a target price, locking in gains.

**Ticker Symbol**
A unique identifier for a publicly traded company (e.g., AAPL for Apple).

**Volume**
The number of shares traded during a given period.

**Volatility**
The degree of price fluctuation. High volatility = large price swings.

## Catalyst Types

**Catalyst**
An event or factor that causes a significant change in a stock's price. Scansmart's primary focus.

**Earnings Catalyst**
Events related to a company's quarterly or annual financial results.

**FDA Catalyst**
U.S. Food and Drug Administration decisions affecting pharmaceutical and biotech companies.
- **PDUFA Date**: FDA decision deadline for drug applications
- **Clinical Trial Results**: Outcomes from drug testing phases
- **Approval/Rejection**: FDA decision on drug applications

**M&A Catalyst (Mergers & Acquisitions)**
- **Acquisition**: One company buying another
- **Merger**: Two companies combining
- **Spinoff**: A company creating a new independent company from part of its business

**Regulatory Catalyst**
Government or regulatory body decisions affecting a company.

**Product Catalyst**
New product launches, updates, or announcements.

**Management Catalyst**
Changes in company leadership (CEO, CFO, board members).

**Financial Catalyst**
Events affecting company finances (debt refinancing, credit rating changes, dividend changes).

**Legal Catalyst**
Lawsuit outcomes, settlements, patent litigation.

**Insider Trading**
When company insiders (executives, directors) buy or sell stock. Can signal confidence or concern.
- **Form 4**: SEC filing reporting insider transactions

## Technical Analysis

**ATR (Average True Range)**
Measures volatility by calculating the average range between high and low prices.

**Bollinger Bands**
Volatility indicator showing upper and lower price bands based on standard deviations.

**Breakout**
When price moves above resistance or below support, often with increased volume.

**Candlestick**
A price chart showing open, high, low, and close for a time period.

**Chart Pattern**
Recognizable formations in price charts that may predict future movements.
- **Head and Shoulders**: Bearish reversal pattern
- **Cup and Handle**: Bullish continuation pattern
- **Triangle**: Consolidation pattern that can break either way
- **Double Top/Bottom**: Reversal patterns

**MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)**
Momentum indicator showing the relationship between two moving averages.

**Moving Average (MA)**
Average price over a specific period, smoothing out short-term fluctuations.
- **SMA (Simple Moving Average)**: Equal weight to all prices
- **EMA (Exponential Moving Average)**: More weight to recent prices

**Momentum**
The rate of price change. High momentum = fast price movement.

**Overbought**
Condition where a stock has risen too far, too fast and may be due for a pullback.

**Oversold**
Condition where a stock has fallen too far, too fast and may be due for a bounce.

**Relative Strength**
How a stock performs compared to a benchmark (e.g., S&P 500).

**Resistance**
Price level where selling pressure historically prevents further price increases.

**RSI (Relative Strength Index)**
Momentum oscillator (0-100) measuring speed and magnitude of price changes.
- Below 30: Oversold
- Above 70: Overbought

**Support**
Price level where buying pressure historically prevents further price decreases.

**Trend**
The general direction of price movement (uptrend, downtrend, sideways).

**Trendline**
A line connecting price points to visualize the trend direction.

**Volume Profile**
Distribution of volume at different price levels.

## Fundamental Analysis

**Balance Sheet**
Financial statement showing assets, liabilities, and equity at a point in time.

**Cash Flow Statement**
Financial statement showing cash inflows and outflows.

**Current Ratio**
Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities. Measures ability to pay short-term obligations.

**Debt-to-Equity Ratio**
Total Debt ÷ Shareholders' Equity. Measures financial leverage.

**Dividend**
Cash payment made to shareholders from company profits.
- **Dividend Yield**: Annual dividend ÷ stock price
- **Payout Ratio**: Dividends ÷ earnings

**Earnings**
Company's net profit, typically reported quarterly and annually.

**Earnings Per Share (EPS)**
Net Income ÷ Outstanding Shares. Profit allocated to each share.

**Earnings Surprise**
Difference between actual earnings and analyst estimates.
- **Beat**: Actual > Expected (positive surprise)
- **Miss**: Actual < Expected (negative surprise)

**EBITDA**
Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. Measures operating performance.

**Free Cash Flow (FCF)**
Cash from operations minus capital expenditures. Available for distribution.

**Gross Margin**
(Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold) ÷ Revenue. Profitability measure.

**Growth Rate**
Rate at which a metric (revenue, earnings, etc.) is increasing.
- **YoY (Year-over-Year)**: Compared to same period last year
- **QoQ (Quarter-over-Quarter)**: Compared to previous quarter

**Income Statement**
Financial statement showing revenues, expenses, and profit over a period.

**Operating Margin**
Operating Income ÷ Revenue. Profitability from core operations.

**P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)**
Stock Price ÷ Earnings Per Share. Valuation metric.
- **Forward P/E**: Uses estimated future earnings
- **Trailing P/E**: Uses past 12 months earnings

**P/B Ratio (Price-to-Book)**
Market Cap ÷ Book Value. Compares price to net asset value.

**P/S Ratio (Price-to-Sales)**
Market Cap ÷ Revenue. Useful for companies without profits.

**PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings to Growth)**
P/E Ratio ÷ Earnings Growth Rate. Factors in growth.

**Revenue**
Total income from sales before expenses (also called "sales" or "top line").

**ROA (Return on Assets)**
Net Income ÷ Total Assets. How efficiently assets generate profit.

**ROE (Return on Equity)**
Net Income ÷ Shareholders' Equity. Return to shareholders.

## Options & Derivatives

**Call Option**
Contract giving the right (not obligation) to buy stock at a specific price by a certain date.

**Put Option**
Contract giving the right (not obligation) to sell stock at a specific price by a certain date.

**Strike Price**
The price at which an option can be exercised.

**Expiration Date**
The date when an option contract expires.

**Premium**
The price paid to buy an option contract.

**In the Money (ITM)**
- Call: Stock price > strike price
- Put: Stock price < strike price

**Out of the Money (OTM)**
- Call: Stock price < strike price
- Put: Stock price > strike price

**Put/Call Ratio**
Put Volume ÷ Call Volume. Can indicate market sentiment.
- High ratio: Bearish sentiment
- Low ratio: Bullish sentiment

**Implied Volatility (IV)**
Market's expectation of future volatility based on option prices.

**Open Interest**
Total number of outstanding option contracts.

**Unusual Options Activity**
Options trading volume significantly higher than normal, possibly indicating informed trading.

## Market Mechanics

**After Hours Trading**
Trading that occurs outside regular market hours (4pm - 8pm ET).

**Pre-Market Trading**
Trading before regular market hours (4am - 9:30am ET).

**Market Hours**
Regular trading hours: 9:30am - 4pm ET (Monday-Friday).

**Circuit Breaker**
Trading halt triggered by large market declines to prevent panic selling.

**Halt**
Temporary suspension of trading in a security.

**Wash Sale**
Selling a security at a loss and repurchasing within 30 days, disallowing the tax loss.

**Settlement Date**
Date when trades are finalized (T+2 for stocks: trade date plus 2 business days).

**Float**
Number of shares available for public trading (excludes insider holdings).

**Short Interest**
Number of shares that have been sold short but not yet covered.

**Dark Pool**
Private exchange where institutional traders execute large orders away from public markets.

## Statistical & AI Terms

**Alpha**
Excess return of an investment relative to a benchmark.

**Beta**
Measure of volatility relative to the market. 1.0 = same as market.

**Sharpe Ratio**
(Return - Risk-Free Rate) ÷ Standard Deviation. Risk-adjusted return metric.

**Standard Deviation**
Statistical measure of price volatility.

**Correlation**
Statistical relationship between two securities (-1 to +1).
- +1: Perfect positive correlation
- 0: No correlation
- -1: Perfect negative correlation

**Maximum Drawdown**
Largest peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value.

**Win Rate**
Percentage of profitable trades.

**Profit Factor**
Gross Profit ÷ Gross Loss. Above 1.0 is profitable.

**Machine Learning (ML)**
AI technique where systems learn from data to make predictions.

**Natural Language Processing (NLP)**
AI technique for understanding and processing human language.

**Sentiment Analysis**
Using NLP to determine emotional tone (positive/negative/neutral).

**Feature Engineering**
Creating input variables for machine learning models.

**Training Data**
Historical data used to teach ML models.

**Prediction Confidence**
Probability that an AI prediction is correct.

## Regulatory & Compliance

**SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission)**
US government agency regulating securities markets.

**FINRA (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority)**
Self-regulatory organization overseeing broker-dealers.

**Form 10-K**
Annual report filed with SEC, comprehensive financial information.

**Form 10-Q**
Quarterly report filed with SEC.

**Form 8-K**
Current report for material events filed with SEC.

**Form 4**
Report of insider trading filed with SEC.

**Accredited Investor**
Individual meeting income/net worth requirements to invest in certain securities.

**Pattern Day Trader (PDT)**
Trader executing 4+ day trades in 5 business days, requires $25K minimum account.

## Risk Management

**Diversification**
Spreading investments across different assets to reduce risk.

**Position Sizing**
Determining how much capital to allocate to each trade.

**Risk/Reward Ratio**
Potential profit ÷ potential loss. 2:1 means risking $1 to make $2.

**Exposure**
Total capital at risk in the market.

**Concentration Risk**
Risk from having too much invested in a single asset or sector.

**Systematic Risk**
Market-wide risk that cannot be diversified away.

**Unsystematic Risk**
Company/sector-specific risk that can be diversified.

**Value at Risk (VaR)**
Maximum expected loss over a time period at a given confidence level.
